How to Compress a PDF to 100KB Without Losing Readability

Published on October 29, 2026 · by AiLovePDF

Some portals are stricter than others. You prepare your documents for an application and the instruction reads: each PDF must be under 100KB — not 500KB, not 200KB, but 100KB. Scholarship schemes, exam portals and many government forms use this tighter limit, and a normal compression pass often lands at 130KB or 150KB: close, but still rejected. The good news is that 100KB is reachable for most form documents without turning the text into a blur — if you work in the right order.

This guide is the stricter companion to our walkthrough on how to compress a PDF to 200KB for online application forms. Below are the exact steps with the free Compress PDF tool, the ladder of extra techniques for when one pass is not enough, and how to check readability before you submit. Everything happens in your browser: your file is never uploaded.

Why 100KB is harder than it looks

A page of typed text weighs almost nothing — the words themselves are just a few kilobytes. Nearly all the weight of a scanned document is image data: the photograph of the paper, captured at high resolution and in full colour. A four-page colour scan at 300 DPI can weigh 4–8MB, so reaching 100KB means shedding around 98% of its weight — and all of it has to come out of the images.

That is why the order of techniques matters. Remove image weight that carries no information first — extra pages, colour where colour is not needed, resolution far beyond what a screen shows — and only then ask the compressor to squeeze what remains. Work that way, and the letters, the part the portal officer actually reads, stay crisp.

Step by step: compress a PDF toward 100KB

Start with the compressor itself, at its strongest setting:

  1. Open the tool. Go to Compress PDF in your browser, on your phone or computer. No account, nothing to install.
  2. Add your PDF. Tap "Select PDF" or drag the file onto the page. It loads instantly, because it is not being uploaded anywhere.
  3. Choose the strongest quality level. For a 100KB target, skip the balanced level and start with maximum compression. You can step back if the result looks too rough.
  4. Compress and check the size. The tool shows the new file size before you download. If it reads 100KB or less, you are done.
  5. Download and verify. Save the file, open it, and zoom to 150% to confirm that names, numbers and dates are still perfectly readable.

If the result is still above the limit — say 160KB — do not keep re-compressing the same file. Each extra pass adds blur and saves less than the one before. Move down the ladder instead.

The ladder: five more ways to reach 100KB

Apply these in order, and stop as soon as you are under the limit:

  • Delete pages the form does not ask for. Blank backs of ID cards, extra certificates, instruction sheets — every removed page is weight gone with zero loss to the pages that remain. Use Delete PDF Pages, then compress the trimmed file.
  • Split the document. If the portal accepts a separate upload per document, split the PDF and compress each part on its own. Three files of 90KB pass a per-file limit that one 270KB file fails.
  • Rescan in grayscale at 150 DPI. If you still have the paper, this is the single biggest saver. Colour is most of a scan’s weight; a grayscale page at 150 DPI can weigh 80–90% less than a colour page at 300 DPI, and printed text stays sharp.
  • Start from the original file. Work from the first scan or the original exported PDF — not a copy forwarded through messaging apps, which adds artefacts without adding information.
  • Drop full-page photographs. If the PDF includes a scanned photo page or a decorative cover the portal does not require, removing it often saves more than any quality setting.

Keeping the text readable at 100KB

A file that fits the limit but cannot be read is worse than a rejected upload: rejection at the verification stage can cost you the whole application round, not just a few minutes. Two rules protect you. First, take the savings from the images, not from the letters — grayscale, sensible resolution and fewer pages shrink a file while the text stays legible. Second, always inspect the result at 150% zoom: your name, your ID or roll number, dates, and any stamp or signature. If the smallest print on the page is still clear, the document is safe to submit.

And keep your original file untouched. Work on a copy, so that if a portal later asks for a higher-quality version, you still have the full scan ready.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can any PDF be compressed to 100KB?⌄

Not every file. A long document full of colour photographs may not reach 100KB while staying readable. But the documents portals ask for — a few pages of forms, certificates and ID scans — usually get there with the strongest compression level plus the ladder in this guide: delete unneeded pages, split per-document uploads, or rescan in grayscale at 150 DPI.

Why does my text look blurry after compressing to 100KB?⌄

Blur appears when the quality level is pushed too far for the amount of image data in the file, or when the same file is compressed again and again. Take the savings from the images instead: remove pages, go grayscale, lower the scan resolution. Then check the result at 150% zoom — names, numbers and dates must be crisp before you submit.

The portal limit is 100KB per file and I have four documents. What should I do?⌄

Compress each document separately and upload them one by one. If the portal instead demands a single combined PDF under 100KB, delete unneeded pages and rescan in grayscale before merging, so the combined document still fits.
