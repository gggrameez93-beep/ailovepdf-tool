How to Merge PDF Files for a Job Application (CV, ID and Certificates in One File)

Published on November 6, 2026 · by AiLovePDF

Most job portals do not want five separate attachments. They give you one upload box and expect one complete file, which is why applications get rejected or ignored when a certificate, ID copy or cover letter is missing. Recruiters also read in order: if your CV arrives last or your certificates are scattered across emails, your application looks careless before anyone reads a word of it.

Learning how to merge pdf files for a job application solves that in minutes. You put the cover letter first, then the CV, then ID and certificates in the exact order an employer expects. With the free how to merge pdf files for a job application tool from AiLovePDF, the combining happens entirely in your browser, so personal documents like passports and diplomas are never uploaded to a server.

What to include, and in what order

Start with what the advert actually asks for, no more and no less. A typical bundle is a short cover letter, a two-page CV, a copy of your ID or passport, degree and training certificates, and reference letters if you have them. Put them in reading order: cover letter, CV, certificates from most recent to oldest, then ID at the end unless the portal specifically asks for ID first.

If some documents are still separate PDFs from a scanner or university portal, that is fine — merging accepts files from different sources. What matters is consistency. Rename your source files clearly before you start, check that scanned pages are upright and legible, and remove anything the employer did not request, such as bank statements or unrelated certificates. A focused five-to-eight-page application beats a twenty-page dump.

  • Cover letter tailored to this specific role
  • Current CV, ideally two pages maximum
  • Degree, diploma and training certificates
  • Copy of ID, passport or work permit if requested
  • Reference letters or portfolio pages, if relevant

Why one merged file gets read more often

Applicant tracking systems and busy hiring managers both reward simplicity. One file means one download, one preview and no risk that an attachment gets filtered out by an email system. For government jobs, university posts and large companies, the portal often has a strict single-file rule anyway, and submitting separate files can mean automatic rejection even when you are well qualified for the role.

Merging also protects your privacy story. Instead of emailing sensitive ID copies to several addresses, you share one intentional file with one recipient. Because AiLovePDF runs 100% in your browser with no sign-up, your documents stay on your device while you build that file. There is no watermark added, so the result looks like something you produced professionally, not a free-tool sample.

Tips for a clean, professional result

Before you merge, open each PDF once. Scanned certificates are often sideways, oversized or faint — fix the source rather than hoping the recruiter will rotate their screen. If one scan is huge, compress it separately first so the final application stays under common portal limits of 2 MB to 5 MB. Keep total length sensible: recruiters skim, so every extra page should earn its place.

After merging, read the final file page by page exactly as a stranger would. Check that pages follow each other logically, that no blank scanned page slipped in, and that your name and contact details appear on the CV. Save the result with a clear filename such as Firstname-Lastname-Application-Role.pdf. That small detail helps your file stay identifiable in a folder of two hundred downloads.

  • Keep the final file under the portal's size limit
  • Use a clear, name-based filename for the merged PDF
  • Delete blank or duplicate scanned pages beforehand
  • Check page order once more before you submit
  • Keep an unmerged copy of each original document

How to merge your application PDFs in minutes

  1. Gather your cover letter, CV, ID and certificates as separate PDF files in one folder.
  2. Open the Merge PDF tool and add at least two files — merging needs two or more PDFs.
  3. Arrange the files in application order: cover letter first, then CV, then certificates and ID.
  4. Start the merge and wait a few seconds while your browser combines the pages.
  5. Download the single merged PDF and open it to check order, rotation and legibility.
  6. Upload that one file to the job portal or attach it to your application email.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it safe to merge documents that include my passport or ID?⌄

With AiLovePDF, yes in the way that matters most: the merging happens locally in your browser and your files are never uploaded to our servers. That reduces exposure, but you should still only send ID copies to employers who genuinely need them and through their official portal.

How many files do I need to use the merge tool?⌄

You need at least two PDF files. There is no benefit in merging a single file with itself. If your CV is one PDF and everything else is another, those two are enough, and you can add more certificates as separate files in the same job.

Will merging change the quality of my scanned certificates?⌄

Merging combines pages without re-encoding them into images, so text and scans normally keep their original quality. If a certificate was blurry in the source scan, merging will not sharpen it. Rescan faint documents at 200 to 300 dpi for a clearer result.

What if the merged application is too large for the portal?⌄

Large colour scans are usually the cause. Compress the merged file or the biggest source scan, and remove pages the advert did not request. Most portals accept 2 MB to 10 MB, so a text CV plus a few sensible scans should fit comfortably.
